# Output: campaign brief

**The prompt, both times:** *"Draft a Q3 campaign brief for Deuce, a booking software company for padel clubs."*

The only difference is that the second one had the context layer to read.

---

# Without the context

## Q3 campaign: grow your club with Deuce

**Objective:** Increase brand awareness and generate qualified leads among padel clubs in Europe.

**Target audience:** Padel club owners and managers looking to modernise their operations and improve the member experience.

**Key message:** Deuce is the complete platform for modern padel clubs. Streamline your bookings, grow your community, and take your club to the next level.

**Channels**

| Channel | Activity |
|---|---|
| Paid social | LinkedIn and Meta campaigns targeting club owners and sports facility managers |
| Content marketing | Weekly blog posts on padel industry trends, SEO-optimised |
| Email | Monthly newsletter with product updates and best practices |
| Webinars | "The Future of Padel Club Management", monthly, with industry guests |
| Partnerships | Explore co-marketing with padel equipment brands and federations |

**Content themes**
- The rise of padel across Europe
- Digital transformation in sports facilities
- Building a thriving club community
- Data-driven decision making for club owners

**Success metrics**
- 40% increase in website traffic
- 500 new email subscribers
- 25% increase in demo requests
- Social media engagement rate above 4%

**Budget:** Recommend allocating across paid (40%), content (30%), events (20%), tools (10%).

# With the context

## Campaign: the Tuesday afternoon problem

**Period:** July to September 2026, before the indoor season starts

**Objective:** Get inbound demo requests from 9 a month to 30. Get inbound from 18% of pipeline to 50%.

**The idea:** Clubs worry about Thursday evening, when everyone wants a court. But Thursday sells itself. The money is really decided on Tuesday afternoon, when the courts are empty and you're still paying for lights, staff and rent.

Nobody in this market talks about empty hours. It's an awkward subject, so everyone sells growth instead. That's the gap.

**Audience:** Owners of independent padel clubs with 4 to 12 courts in Flanders and the Netherlands. About 600 people. We can name most of them.

**The message:** Your Tuesday afternoon is where the money is. Here's what other clubs did about it.

**Channels**

| Channel | What we do there | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Club visits and owner events | Sofie goes to about six a month | Converts better than anything else we've tried. Half the budget. |
| LinkedIn, Sofie's account | One post a week. One observation each time, no sales pitch. | It reaches the 600 owners. That's all we want from it. |
| Website | One page explaining what your empty hours cost you, with a calculator | Somewhere for people to land |
| Customer clubs | Three short case studies with real numbers on off-peak bookings | We started tracking this in June, so now we can prove it |

**What we're not doing:** paid social (we tried it for three months, got one customer, stopped in May), anything aimed at players rather than owners, Spain, and clubs with fewer than four courts.

**How we'll know it worked**

| Metric | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound demo requests | 9/mo | 30/mo |
| Inbound share of pipeline | 18% | 50% |
| Off-peak bookings in customer clubs | +6pts | +12pts |

**Risks**
- Sofie is doing the club visits, the posts and the sales calls. That won't hold past September. Write and schedule the posts in July.
- Clubs buy in August or they wait until January. If something lands late September, we've missed the season.

# What changed, and why

The first brief isn't stupid. It's what any competent marketer would produce knowing nothing about the company. That's the problem: it would work for any booking software company in any sport in any country.

| The first version says | The context says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Run paid social on LinkedIn and Meta | We tried paid social for three months, got one customer, stopped in May | `goals.md` → change log |
| Target padel clubs across Europe | 600 named owners in Flanders and the Netherlands, 4 to 12 courts | `business-context.md` → ICP |
| Content about the rise of padel | Don't talk about padel's growth as if we had anything to do with it | `brand-guidelines.md` → what we avoid |
| Monthly webinars with industry guests | Half the budget goes on Sofie visiting clubs, because that's what converts | `goals.md` → constraints |
| Grow your community, take your club to the next level | Both phrases are on the banned list | `brand-guidelines.md` → banned words |
| Measure traffic, subscribers, engagement rate | Follower counts are explicitly not a goal | `goals.md` → what we're not chasing |
| No topic, just channels | The whole campaign is one idea about empty Tuesdays | `expert-pov.md` → core belief 1 |
| No mention of timing | Clubs buy in August or wait until January | `goals.md` → constraints |
| No risks | Sofie is the bottleneck and will run out of time | `goals.md` → constraints, people |

The second brief cost the same one sentence of prompt. The difference is that someone had already written down what they'd learned.
