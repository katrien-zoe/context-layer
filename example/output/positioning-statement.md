# Output: positioning statement

**The prompt, both times:** *"Write a positioning statement for Deuce, booking software for padel clubs."*

The only difference is that the second one had the context layer to read.

---

# Without the context

Deuce is the complete platform for modern padel clubs.

We help club owners streamline their operations, grow their community, and unlock the full potential of their venue. From effortless booking to member management and insights, everything you need is in one place.

Unlike traditional marketplaces, Deuce puts you in control of your brand and your customer relationships. No hidden fees, no compromises.

Whether you run a single club or a growing group, Deuce scales with you.

Ready to take your club to the next level?

---

# With the context

Padel clubs have two problems. On Thursday evening, three people want the same court. On Tuesday afternoon, nobody wants any of them.

Most clubs fix this by joining a marketplace like Playtomic. It sends you players, and it fills some of those empty hours. That part works.

What you give up is the customer. The player books through their app, so they have an account with the marketplace, not with you. You get the booking. You don't get the email address.

Deuce is booking software for clubs that want to keep their own member list. Your name on the app, your branding, your data. You can export all of it whenever you want, on any plan.

It costs €149 a month no matter how many bookings you take. There's no commission, so when your club grows, you keep it.

One thing: if your club is new and your courts are empty, stay on the marketplace. You need players more than you need a mailing list. Come back when you're busy.

---

# What changed, and why

The first version could be about any booking software for any kind of venue. Swap "padel clubs" for "yoga studios" and nothing breaks. That's the test it fails.

| The first version says | The context says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| "The complete platform for modern padel clubs" | Banned. If a competitor could put it on their homepage, it isn't ours. | `brand-guidelines.md` → banned words |
| "Streamline", "effortless", "unlock the full potential" | All banned | `brand-guidelines.md` → banned words |
| "Scales with you", single club or growing group | We don't sell to chains over 8 locations or clubs under 4 courts | `business-context.md` → anti-ICP |
| "Unlike traditional marketplaces" | Name Playtomic. Owners know who we mean. | `competitors.md` → profiles |
| "No hidden fees" | €149 a month. Say the number. | `business-context.md` → differentiator 3 |
| Describes features | Describe the Tuesday afternoon before the feature that fixes it | `brand-guidelines.md` → how we write |
| Ends with "take your club to the next level" | Ends by telling new clubs to stay with the competitor | `business-context.md` → anti-ICP |
| Says nothing a competitor couldn't say | Every claim has a number or a name attached | `brand-guidelines.md` → how we write |

The last paragraph is the one that matters. Telling some readers not to buy yet is the sort of thing no prompt asks for and no generic version produces. It came from an anti-ICP written by someone who had already lost money learning it.
