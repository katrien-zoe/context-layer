# Output: LinkedIn post

**The prompt, both times:** *"Draft a LinkedIn post for Sofie, founder of Deuce, about padel clubs and marketplaces."*

The only difference is that the second one had the context layer to read.

---

# Without the context

Padel is booming across Europe. 🎾

But here's what nobody's talking about: many club owners are unknowingly giving away their most valuable asset.

When you rely on a marketplace for bookings, you're not just paying commission. You're handing over the customer relationship, the data, and ultimately control of your own business.

At Deuce, we believe club owners should own their community. That's why we built a platform that puts you back in control.

Your club. Your members. Your data.

The clubs that thrive over the next 5 years will be the ones that own their customer relationships today.

What do you think? Are marketplaces helping or hurting independent clubs? 👇

---

# With the context

I used Playtomic at my club for three years and it worked. My courts were full. I'd have had a much harder time without it.

Then I wanted to start a members' league.

To do that I needed to email everyone who played regularly. About 300 people. I couldn't. I didn't have their email addresses. Playtomic did.

That's the deal you make when you join a marketplace. They send you players you wouldn't have found on your own. In return, those players have an account with them, not with you. You get the booking. They get the customer.

If your club is new and half your courts are empty, take that deal. You need players more than you need a mailing list.

But once you're full, look at what you're paying. You're giving away a percentage on bookings from people who would have come anyway. And you still can't contact them.

It took me three years to notice. Might be worth checking where you are with it.

# What changed, and why

The first post is a competitor attack written by a company. The second is a founder telling you what happened to her.

| The first version does | The context says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Opens with "padel is booming" | Don't talk about padel's growth as if we had anything to do with it | `brand-guidelines.md` → what we avoid |
| Says owners are "unknowingly" giving something away | Marketplaces are the right choice for a new club. Say so. | `expert-pov.md` → core belief 2 |
| Pitches the product in paragraph four | Social posts are one observation, no sales pitch | `brand-guidelines.md` → tone by channel |
| "Your club. Your members. Your data." | That's the tagline, not a LinkedIn post | `brand-guidelines.md` → tagline |
| Predicts what happens over five years | She never talks about the future of the sport | `expert-pov.md` → would never say |
| Ends with "What do you think? 👇" | No call to action. She doesn't ask for engagement. | `brand-guidelines.md` → social tone |
| Emojis | Not her | `expert-pov.md` → voice notes |
| No story | She always starts with something that happened | `expert-pov.md` → tell |

The thing that makes the second post work is that it admits the competitor was useful. That admission is written down in `expert-pov.md`, because it's what Sofie actually thinks. Nothing in the prompt would have produced it.
