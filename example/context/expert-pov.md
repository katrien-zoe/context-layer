# Expert POV & founder voice

*Filled from a long conversation with Sofie, plus her LinkedIn posts and a podcast she did for a Belgian sports business show in February.*

## The one big idea

> Padel clubs created the boom, and most of them ended up not owning their own customers. That isn't a software problem. It's a deal that looked free when they signed it.

## Core beliefs

- **Empty Tuesdays decide whether a club makes money.** Everyone worries about peak hours, but peak hours fill themselves. What matters is whether anything happens between eleven and four, when the costs run anyway.
- **Marketplaces are the right choice for a new club.** If your courts are empty, take the deal. It stops being a good deal once you're full, usually after about eighteen months.
- **Owners don't realise what the member list is worth** until they try to start a league or sell the club, and find they can't email anyone.
- **People come for their friends, not the court.** Most players turn up with the same three people every week and have a drink afterwards. Software that treats this as renting a court by the hour misses the point.

## Contrarian takes

| The consensus view | Our take |
|---|---|
| Clubs need more players | Most clubs need their existing players to come more often. That's cheaper than finding new ones. |
| Commission is fair because you only pay when you earn | You pay more as you grow, and the money goes to someone else. A flat fee means growing is good for you. |
| Charging different prices at different times feels unfair | Charging less for hours nobody wants is normal. Cinemas and airlines have done it for decades. |
| Clubs should work on the customer experience | The experience is usually fine already. That's why people come back. Work on Tuesday afternoon instead. |

## Origin story

Sofie opened a six-court club outside Antwerp in 2020, just as padel took off, and it filled up. She used Playtomic from the first month and it worked. She always says that part out loud.

Three years later she wanted to start a members' league. To do that she needed to email her regular players. She couldn't. About 300 people had been coming to her club every week for years and all she had were their first names on a grid on the wall. Their email addresses were in Playtomic's system.

She sold the club in 2025 and built what she wished she'd had, with Tom, who had heard the story about forty times by then.

## Themes we own

- **The Tuesday afternoon problem.** Empty hours, off-peak pricing, and why quiet times decide whether a club makes money.
- **Who owns the customer.** What you give up when you join a marketplace, described honestly, including when it's worth it.
- **Running a club as a business.** Most software in this market treats owners as caretakers of a venue. They're small business owners who happen to have a community.

## Voice notes for ghost-writing

- **Signature phrases:** "look", "the honest answer is", "that's fine, until", "nobody wants 11am"
- **Rhythm:** conversational. Gets to the point by the second sentence. Admits the weakness in her own argument before anyone else can.
- **Would never say:** "passionate", "excited to share", "thrilled", "the future of", anything with three exclamation marks or one
- **Tell:** she agrees with the other side first, then says her bit. It's why people believe her on calls.
- **Sample of her real writing:**
  > "Playtomic filled my courts for three years and I'd have struggled without it. Then I tried to start a members' league and found I couldn't email my own regulars. Both of those are true, which is what makes it a hard thing to talk about."
