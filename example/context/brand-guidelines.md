# Brand guidelines

**Purpose.** So Deuce sounds the same whether it's the website, an invoice reminder, or Sofie replying to a WhatsApp at nine on a Sunday.

**About the brand.** Founded 2025 by Sofie Claes, who ran a six-court club near Antwerp, and Tom Beeckman, who built the first version because Sofie kept describing the same problem at dinner.

---

# 1. Brand core

**Our mission**
> Let club owners run a full club without handing the members to someone else.

**Our vision**
> Padel grows up, and the clubs that built it still own what they built.

**Our brand values**
- **We're on the owner's side.** Before we build something, we ask whether it makes the club stronger or just makes them need us more.
- **Say the awkward thing, kindly.** Sometimes the honest answer is "you're too small for us" or "stay with Playtomic another year".
- **Don't be too serious about the sport.** It's a game people play badly on Wednesday nights. That's part of why we like it.

**Our team values**
- Go and play at a customer's club before you redesign their booking flow
- Sunday emails get answered Monday, and that's fine
- If Sofie wouldn't say it on a call, we don't put it on the website

**Brand personality in three words**
> warm · direct · slightly amused

# 2. Visual identity

## Logo

**Primary logo.** Full wordmark. Use for first introductions and anywhere the brand isn't known.
- The shape: the wordmark with the two Es sharing a net line
- The symbol: deuce, the moment the game refuses to end
- The style: rounded, confident, not sporty-aggressive

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use the mark alone in-app and on avatars | Use the mark alone for first introductions |
| Keep clear space equal to the D height | Stretch, rotate or add motion lines |
| Use on court green or chalk white | Place over photography of a match |

## Colour

**Core colours** (65 to 75% of any layout)

| Colour | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Near black | #141414 | Headlines, body copy |
| Chalk white | #FBF9F4 | Backgrounds, long-form |
| Court green | #2D5F4C | Primary brand colour, UI, recurring elements |

**Secondary:** Electric blue #4B6BFB. One per composition.
**Accent:** Ball orange #FF5C39. Under 5% of a layout. CTAs and one thing you want someone to notice.

**Colour usage principles**
- Default to near black, chalk white and court green
- Orange is for one thing per screen, not for enthusiasm
- Never more than three brand colours in a layout
- Long-form text only on chalk white or near black

## Typography

| Use case | Font |
|---|---|
| Website and brand headlines | GT Walsheim (Medium) |
| Body copy | GT Walsheim |
| Office (PowerPoint, Word, email) | Verdana |

## Photography

Real clubs, real members, mid-rally or mid-conversation. Slightly unflattering is fine. Someone is usually laughing at someone.

**Never:** stock photography, professional athletes, anyone high-fiving in slow motion, drone shots of empty courts at golden hour.

# 3. Brand messaging

**Tagline**
> Your club, your members.

**Value proposition**
> Booking and membership software for padel clubs that would rather not rent their own community back from a marketplace.

## Voice

**Brand voice:** warm, direct, slightly amused.

That means:
- We talk to owners like people running a business, because that's what they are.
- We say the awkward thing, including when it's that we're not right for them.
- We find the sport funny, in a fond way. It's a game where grown adults argue about whether a ball touched a wall.
- We don't oversell. If something is fine but nothing special, we say it's fine.

**How we sound.** If Deuce were a person, it would be:
- The club owner two towns over who tells you what things really cost
- Dry, but never unkind about the members
- Clear first, funny second, and never funny instead of clear

> We don't sell a dream, we don't talk about the future of the sport, and we never call anything effortless.

## Tone variations

| Channel | Tone | Sample copy |
|---|---|---|
| Product and UX copy | Short, plain, occasionally amused | "Court 3 is booked. Try 11:00, nobody wants 11:00." |
| Sales and marketing | Direct. Say what the trade actually is. | "A marketplace fills your courts. It also keeps your customers." |
| Thought leadership | Honest, a bit pointed, still fond of the sport | "Padel grew faster than anyone's paperwork could keep up with. That's most of the problem." |
| Customer support | Human, quick, takes the blame | "Ours, not yours. Fixed, and here's what happened." |
| Internal | Direct, no ceremony | "This slipped. New date is the 14th." |
| Social | One observation, dry, no pitch | "Every club has a Tuesday afternoon problem and a Thursday evening problem. They are not the same problem." |

*Rule of thumb for social: if a club owner would read it and think "that's my week", it fits. If it just sounds like marketing, it doesn't.*

## How we write

- Write to one owner, not to "clubs". Use "you" and "your members".
- Say "we", not "the platform".
- Describe the Tuesday afternoon before you describe the feature that fixes it.
- Use numbers instead of adjectives. "11% of what you could have earned" beats "significant".
- Short sentences. If a sentence has two ideas in it, make it two sentences.
- No word anyone would need to look up.
- Sentence case for headlines and titles.
- British / EU English.
- No em dashes.
- Jokes are fine. Puns about tennis scoring are not, we've used the name already.

**Examples**
- ✅ "Tuesday at 2pm your club is empty and you already know it." ❌ "Optimise your court utilisation."
- ✅ "€149 a month. That's the whole price." ❌ "Flexible pricing to suit clubs of every size."

## What we avoid

- Hype, and using "community" as if it's a number
- Talking about padel's growth as if we had anything to do with it
- Explaining the sport to people who run clubs
- Anything a generic booking company could also say

**Especially avoid:** "revolutionise", "seamless", "elevate your club", "the future of padel", "game changer", "effortless booking", "the complete platform".

> If Playtomic could put it on their homepage, it isn't ours.

## Pre-publish checklist

1. Would a club owner recognise their own week in this?
2. Does it help them decide something?
3. Could it be shorter?
4. Is it honest, including about what we don't do?

*If the answer isn't clear, rewrite.*
