# example/

The six templates from this repo, filled in for a made-up company. Plus three things written from them, each shown twice: once without the context, once with.

**Deuce is made up.** A small Belgian company selling booking software to padel clubs. Its main competitor is a marketplace that fills empty courts but keeps the customer details. Invented so this example can be specific without putting words in a real company's mouth.

## Start here

Open [`output/campaign-brief.md`](output/campaign-brief.md).

Same prompt twice. One sentence: *"Draft a Q3 campaign brief for Deuce, a booking software company for padel clubs."*

The first version recommends paid social, monthly webinars, and content about the rise of padel across Europe. It's fine. Any competent marketer would produce it knowing nothing about the company.

The second one knows that paid social was tried for three months and stopped in May. That the audience is 600 named owners in Flanders and the Netherlands. That clubs buy in August or they wait until January. And that Sofie is doing too much and will run out of time by September.

Nobody typed any of that into the prompt. It was already written down.

## The rest

- [`output/linkedin-post.md`](output/linkedin-post.md): a founder post, generic version and real version
- [`output/positioning-statement.md`](output/positioning-statement.md): same
- [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) and [`context/`](context/): the filled-in files everything above reads from

Filling in the context took about an afternoon. It's the kind of thing you'd put together from one long conversation with the founder, the website, a few sales calls, and an hour looking at competitors.

## What this doesn't show

Writing things down doesn't make the thinking good.

The idea about empty Tuesday afternoons came from someone who ran a club for four years and watched the lights burn over empty courts. Nothing generates that for you.

What writing it down does is make sure it's still there four months later, in a campaign brief, instead of having been said once on a call and forgotten.

Same with the uncomfortable parts. The positioning statement ends by telling some readers to stay with the competitor for now. That came from an anti-ICP written by someone who had already lost money finding out the hard way.
